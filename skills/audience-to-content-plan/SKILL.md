# Audience to Content Plan

Help a solo creator turn what they already know into a small, testable content plan. Do not pretend that a short brief is audience research.

## Ask for or identify the inputs

Use available information first. If essential details are missing, ask concise questions about:

- Who the creator wants to reach.
- The creator's topic, experience, and point of view.
- The main channel and realistic publishing capacity.
- Any existing audience questions, analytics, interviews, or source material.
- The creator's goal for this planning period.

If the creator cannot answer, proceed with clearly labeled assumptions and keep the plan small.

## Workflow

1. Summarize the audience and goal in plain language. Separate user-provided facts, evidence from supplied materials, and assumptions.
2. Identify up to three content pillars. Tie each pillar to a specific audience need and the creator's demonstrated knowledge.
3. Propose a one-week plan sized to the creator's stated capacity. Do not default to daily publishing.
4. For each idea, include:
   - working title and intended audience;
   - the one useful takeaway;
   - suggested format and channel;
   - the creator's experience, source, or example needed;
   - a small response signal to observe (for example, saves, replies, or completion rate).
5. Mark factual claims that need verification. Never invent audience statistics, search trends, or performance forecasts.
6. End with one low-effort experiment and a review question for the end of the week.

## Output format

### Planning assumptions

List facts, supplied evidence, and assumptions separately.

### Content pillars

For each pillar: audience need, creator perspective, and example topic.

### One-week plan

Use a compact table with day, title, format, takeaway, source/material needed, and response signal.

### One experiment

State what to vary, what to observe, and what result would change the next plan. Do not promise reach or growth.

## Quality checks

- Each idea serves the stated audience rather than chasing a generic trend.
- The plan fits the creator's time and production capacity.
- Assumptions and unverified claims are visible.
- No claim about demand or likely performance is stated as fact without evidence.
